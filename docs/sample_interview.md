# Practice interview: Khalid

Fit-out subcontractor, Industrial Area.

## Conversation

**Banker:** Thanks for coming in, Khalid. What brings you in today?

**Khalid:** I'm here because I need a loan. My business is growing and I need the funding to take on some bigger contracts.

**Banker:** How do your main contractors usually pay you?

**Khalid:** They pay me 90 to 120 days after I send the invoice. The real issue here is cash flow, not a lack of work.

**Banker:** What are your biggest costs each month?

**Khalid:** My biggest cost is my 38 workers. Twice this year, I almost couldn't pay them on time through the Wage Protection System because the contractors were late paying me.

**Banker:** Our Business Overdraft would let you draw cash any time to cover wages. Would that help?

**Khalid:** I need to know if this is Sharia-compliant first. I do not accept any products with interest.

**Banker:** Is there anything about how you bank that matters to you?

**Khalid:** I'm here because I need a loan to handle these new, larger contracts. I need to make sure the funding is available when I need it.

**Banker:** We also have Receivables Finance, where we advance up to 80% of an approved invoice and you repay when the contractor pays. How does that sound?

**Khalid:** That sounds like it would help with the cash flow problem. Is this Sharia-compliant?

## Summary

Khalid's main contractors pay him 90 to 120 days after the invoice, so cash runs short when 38 wages are due through WPS. He nearly missed the deadline twice this year. He only accepts Sharia-compliant products, so anything with interest is out.

## Products recommended

- **Receivables Finance**: Turns his unpaid contractor invoices into cash before wages are due, and it is Islamic.
- **Murabaha Working Capital**: Extra working capital for the bigger contracts.

## Coach feedback

Needs found: 3 of 4. Hints used: 1.

- Found: Late payments. Main contractors pay 90 to 120 days after the invoice. The real problem is cash flow, not growth. (you asked: "How do your main contractors usually pay you?")
- Found: Nearly missed paying wages. Twice this year he nearly missed paying his 38 workers on time through the Wage Protection System, because the contractors had not paid him yet. (you asked: "What are your biggest costs each month?")
- Found: Sharia only. He will only accept Sharia-compliant products. Interest is not acceptable to him. (you asked: "Our Business Overdraft would let you draw cash any time to cover wages. Would that help?")
- Missed: Supplier debt. He owes his suppliers about QAR 120,000 and is embarrassed about it. Try: Are there any outstanding balances or debts you currently owe to your suppliers?

**Your picks**

- Receivables Finance: fits. It provides immediate cash from invoices to solve his 90-120 day payment delay.
- Murabaha Working Capital: does not fit. The product only funds goods, but Khalid specifically needs to pay worker wages.

**What went well**

- You successfully identified the cash flow gap caused by the 90 to 120 day payment delay.
- You correctly identified that the client requires Sharia-compliant products only.
- You correctly identified the payroll pressure regarding the 38 workers and the Wage Protection System.

**What was missed**

- You missed the supplier debt of QAR 120,000 which may require additional financing.
- You recommended a product that cannot be used for his primary need of paying wages.

**How to improve**

- Avoid suggesting the Business Overdraft before confirming the client's Sharia requirements.
- Ensure the product limitations, like 'funds specific purchases only,' align with the client's specific needs.
- Include the supplier debt in your final summary to provide a complete picture of his liabilities.
